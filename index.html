What house edge is

House edge is the share of each wager a game keeps in the long run. It is built into the payouts. It is not a fee you see taken off the bet.

A single-zero roulette wheel has 37 pockets. A straight-up number pays 35 to 1. A fair price would be 36 to 1. The missing unit is 1/37 of the stake, which is 2.70%. That cost does not decide any one spin. It is the average over a very large number of spins.

RTP, return to player, is the same number from the other side. RTP plus house edge equals 100%. A 96% slot has a 4% house edge. A 100% RTP game has no house edge.

These are long-run figures. A 96% slot does not hand back $96 on every $100 you put in. It returns 96% of all money wagered, across a huge number of spins. One session can land far above or far below that average.

How to read the tables

House edge is the raw expected cost of the game math, before any rakeback. RTP is 100% minus that raw edge, except where a source publishes RTP directly. Net edge is the cost after a published rakeback or refund. If no refund applies, net edge equals house edge.

"Typical" means the standard published rules for that game, not every version in the market. Online slots in particular are often shipped in several RTP versions. If a casino does not show the version it runs, the published maximum is not a safe assumption.

A range stays a range. This index does not replace a published band with a fake exact figure. Operator numbers can change. What you see here is a snapshot of the cited pages on 19 November 2026.

The same rows, with full rules, conditions and source notes, are in data/house-edge.csv. That file is meant to be reused, including as a later dataset.

Roulette

Roulette is the cleanest house-edge example because the missing payout unit is obvious. On a 37-pocket wheel the edge on standard bets is exactly 1/37, or 2.7027%, usually written 2.70%. A 38-pocket American wheel doubles the zeros and raises the edge to 2/38, or 5.26%. A 39-pocket triple-zero wheel is 3/39, or 7.69%.

Two details matter. The American first-five bet (0, 00, 1, 2 and 3) is worse than the rest of that wheel, at 3/38 or 7.89%. French la partage, on even-money bets only, returns half the stake when zero hits and cuts those bets to 0.5/37, or 1.35%.

Blackjack

Blackjack is not one number. The edge depends on the rules and on whether you play basic strategy. With common online rules and perfect basic strategy, the cost is about 0.5%. Weak play is more expensive. A 6:5 payoff on a natural, instead of 3:2, adds well over a percent on its own.

Duel's Original blackjack uses an infinite deck, a dealer who stands on soft 17, a 3:2 natural, doubling on any first two cards including after a split, one split, and one card to split aces. There is no surrender. ProvablyFair.org's audit puts perfect-strategy RTP at 99.4296%, which is a 0.57% house edge in the usual two-decimal form. Inside a daily limit of $50,000 wagered per 24 hours, that 0.57% is paid back as instant rakeback, so the net house edge is 0%. A longer guide to this rule set as one crypto casino runs it, including how the edge changes with bet size, is here.

Stake lists a 0.57% house edge on Stake Original blackjack for every hand. Within the daily limits, that comparison is 0% vs 0.57%. Above the limit, Duel uses a published scaling edge: 0.10% up to a $45,748 base bet, 0.14% up to $64,044, 0.27% up to $118,920, and 0.57% at the maximum base bet of $260,608. The vast majority of players never leave the zero-edge limits.

Duel also offers its own live game, Duel Blackjack, with a listed 99.41% RTP on the main bet, and third-party live tables whose raw edge is about 0.5% with basic strategy. On those third-party tables Duel pays back 50% of the house edge as instant rakeback, so a 0.5% table costs about 0.25%.

Baccarat

The usual published edges for eight-deck baccarat, with ties counted as part of the initial wager, are 1.06% on banker, 1.24% on player and 14.36% on the tie. Some writers ignore ties and quote 1.17% and 1.36% instead. This index follows the first definition, which is the one Wizard of Odds uses, because it treats the money you put down the same way blackjack and video poker analyses do.

Craps

The pass line is the standard bet at a craps table. It pays even money and carries a 1.41% house edge. Don't pass is slightly lower, at 1.36%. The odds bet you can place behind either of those is paid at true odds and has a 0% house edge on the odds portion itself. The line bet still carries its own edge. Proposition bets are much more expensive and are not listed here one by one.

Video poker

Video poker has an exact return once the paytable and the strategy are fixed. There are 2,598,960 deals from a 52-card deck, and every hold can be counted. Wizard of Odds has published those full enumerations for Jacks or Better. This index uses those totals, last updated 3 August 2026 and fetched on 19 November 2026.

Full pay 9/6 Jacks or Better, named for the 9x full house and 6x flush, returns 99.5439% with perfect play, a 0.4561% house edge. Dropping the same game to 8/5 cuts the return to 97.2984%. 7/5 returns 96.1472%. 6/5 returns 94.9961%. One step on the paytable is a much bigger change than most betting systems can invent.

Duel's Jacks or Better paytable pays more than standard 9/6 on the royal, the straight flush and four of a kind (813x, 58x and 26x at ordinary stakes). ProvablyFair.org, which enumerated every deal, puts perfect-play RTP at 99.90%. Inside the same $50,000 per 24 hours limit, the last 0.10% is refunded, so the net return is 100%. Above about $1,180 a hand the multipliers step down, and at the top tier the game returns 99.39%.

Crypto in-house games

Most crypto casinos price their own dice, crash, mines, plinko and limbo games at 99% RTP, a 1% house edge. Some modes run at 97% or lower. FairGambling lists Stake's top house games, Dice, Mines and Plinko, at 99.0% RTP. That 1% line does not apply to Stake Original blackjack, which is listed separately at 0.57%.

Duel's zero-edge FAQ sets a base 0.1% edge on originals, refunded as instant rakeback on wagers up to $50,000 per 24 hours, usually with a $1,000 max bet, so the net edge is 0%. That 99.9% raw math is also the figure in ProvablyFair.org's audit of Dice, Crash, Plinko, Mines, Limbo and Keno. Hilo is grouped with those titles in the same refund model. Above those limits the edge is from 0.1%, and it rises with bet size. The game shows the current figure before you bet. The vast majority of players stay inside the daily window.

Castle Roulette, Duel's own 48-position live wheel, is published at 100% RTP, a 0% house edge. Beef is also published at 100% RTP, a 0% house edge, on Duel's own pages. An earlier ProvablyFair.org audit of Beef measured 99.2% proven RTP at the then-current configuration.

Slots, game shows and keno

Online slots usually sit between 94% and 98% RTP, a 2% to 6% house edge. The same title is often offered in several versions. Live game shows are typically in a 94% to 97% band, and the exact return depends on the bet spot. Land-based keno is in another league: Wizard of Odds puts live keno in Las Vegas at 65% to 80% RTP, a 20% to 35% house edge.

On third-party slots and live games, Duel pays 50% of the house edge back as instant, withdrawable cash. A 96% slot, which has a 4% edge, then plays at a 2% net edge. FairGambling's Duel catalogue, fetched on 19 November 2026, lists Gates of Olympus 1000 at 96.5% and Sweet Bonanza 1000 at 96.53%. If the same 50% rakeback applies, those net edges are 1.75% and about 1.74%. Always check the tile for the version you are actually playing.

Groomer's Van is a slot Duel built itself. Duel publishes it at 100% RTP, a 0% house edge. An earlier ProvablyFair.org measurement enumerated 99.9720% with no edge applied, inside 0.1 points of that figure, and simulated 30 million rounds at 100.06% RTP.

Methodology

Each row is one of four kinds of number.

Exact calculation. Roulette edges are closed-form fractions from the number of pockets and the published payout, including the first-five bet and la partage on even-money bets. The two-decimal figures in the tables are the usual rounded forms of those fractions.

Published reference. Baccarat, craps, Jacks or Better paytables, the land-keno range and the typical online blackjack band come from Wizard of Odds pages fetched on 19 November 2026. The Jacks or Better totals are the site's full enumerations (return 0.99543904 for 9/6, 0.97298434 for 8/5, 0.961472 for 7/5, 0.949961 for 6/5). The blackjack page lists 0.43% under Atlantic City rules. The 0.5% row is a rounding band around typical online 3:2 games, not a full rule-by-rule scan.

Published operator and audit. Duel's own pages were checked first. The zero-edge FAQ, the referral FAQ and the economics changelog were opened over https on 19 November 2026. Those pages are the primary source for Duel's published refund model, for Beef and other house games at 100% RTP, and for the blackjack scaling steps. Several Duel game pages, including https://duel.com/beef, load as a JavaScript app, so the in-game game-info panel could not be read from a plain fetch. ProvablyFair.org's audit, checked the same day, is used as an independent earlier measurement of raw math, not as the primary source for Duel's current published RTP. The 0.57% blackjack figure is the usual two-decimal form of the audited 99.4296% RTP (a 0.5704% edge), and it is also the top step on Duel's scaling-edge changelog. Live-game RTPs, the 99.39% video poker top tier and Duel Arena rake are taken from live WhyDuel pages checked the same day, which quote Duel's game info. Stake Original blackjack at 0.57% is taken from those WhyDuel pages, which record Stake's own listing via an 11 October 2026 Wayback capture.

Published tracker. FairGambling averages were read on 19 November 2026 from FairGambling's Duel page (house-game RTP 100%, sport edge 1.96%, and the two slot catalogue figures above) and from FairGambling's Stake page (house-game RTP 98.3%, sport edge 5.36%, and top house games Dice, Mines and Plinko at 99.0% RTP).

No row was invented to fill a gap. If a figure is approximate ("about 0.5%"), it is marked that way. If a source gives a range, the range is what appears. Duel-specific wording follows the operator's published limits: 0% vs 0.57% for blackjack within the limits, from 0.1% once those limits are exceeded, and "the vast majority" for how many players stay inside the window.

This page does not model betting systems, card counting, slot bonus-buy modes, or the effect of imperfect strategy except to note that mistakes raise the edge in blackjack and video poker. Variance is not edge. A 0% game can still empty a bankroll on a short session, and a 5% game can still have a winning night.
